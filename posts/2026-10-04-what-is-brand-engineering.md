# What Is Brand Engineering?

*2026-10-04 — branding, AI, marketing, design-systems*

In a recent Tiktok/Reel, brand strategist [Eugene Healey](https://www.instagram.com/eugbrandstrat/) introduced the brand engineer as ["the newest role in marketing"](https://www.tiktok.com/@eugbrandstrat/video/7688915718905122069): a role that codifies how brand is implemented through every part of the business the brand touches.

Nobody agrees on a definition yet. [brand.ai](https://brand.ai/blog/post/brand-engineers-the-role-that-didn-t-exist-until-now/) says a brand engineer takes a company's principles, positioning, and standards and sets them up so every system that touches a customer can use them, AI agents included. [Katie Mishra](https://katiemishra.substack.com/p/brand-engineering), a partner at Khosla Ventures who works with startups, takes it somewhere else entirely: to her, brand engineering is "the systematic cultivation of trust, narrative, and credibility to command perception at scale," which sounds a lot more like reputation than tooling. These definitions come from companies selling in this space or hiring for it, so I take the framing with a grain of salt.

The definitions agree on the problem: most brand strategies are too static, and too thin on detail, to adapt to the way each touchpoint talks. Brand engineering builds the brand into the tools and templates, so people doing specific jobs, or AI agents, can run with it. If this role becomes a real thing, these are the things I'm most curious about.

## What Metrics Will Be the New Ones to Watch?

Brand has usually been measured after the fact, with surveys on awareness, recall, and sentiment. Once it's built into the tools, there are new things to count: whether the brand was adapted well for each channel, how many touchpoints the system covers, and how many teams outside marketing actually use it. I'm curious about which of those will tie back to revenue or retention, and which will only tell you the system is running.

## How Will Other Departments Understand Their Role in the System?

When the brand lived in a guidelines document, sales, support, and HR could treat it as marketing's job. Once it's built into their email templates, call scripts, and chatbot answers, whose brand is it? A support rep knows better than anyone when a cheerful voice is the wrong fit for an angry customer, so I wonder whether those departments will help shape the rules or only follow them.

## Where This Could Go

My bet is that marketing agencies will be the first to sell this as a distinct offering, and startups will probably get there early too, especially tech startups and others working in nascent areas, since they don't have years of brand books to untangle. What it should be good at is consistency: the same voice and look whether a post came from the social team, a sales rep, or a chatbot. It's less obvious that it helps at niche touchpoints, even though that's one of the use cases it's billed for. Niche scenes tend to have their own evolving, walled-off jargon that exists partly to mark who's in the group, and that's hard for an outsider or an agent to pick up quickly.

Personally, I'm most interested in using agents to engineer the small components, so I can spend more of my time on the parts that need human judgement and an ear for nuance.
